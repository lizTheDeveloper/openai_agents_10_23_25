# Holographic Principle

## Definition
The **holographic principle** is a conjecture in theoretical physics that suggests all the information contained within a volume of space can be represented as a theory located on the boundary of that space, often a lower-dimensional space. In simple terms, it proposes that our three-dimensional universe (plus time) could be understood as information encoded on a two-dimensional surface. 

## Historical Origins
- Introduced by Gerard 't Hooft in 1993, motivated by black hole thermodynamics and quantum gravity puzzles.
- Leonard Susskind gave the principle its current form within the context of string theory.

## Motivation: Black Hole Entropy
- Black holes paradoxically have entropy proportional not to their volume, but to the surface area of their event horizon. (Bekenstein–Hawking entropy)
- This hints that the fundamental degrees of freedom in a region are encoded on its boundary.

## Key Quotes and Sources
- “The maximum entropy inside a region scales with the surface area, not the volume...” ([Wikipedia: Holographic principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holographic_principle))
- For a black hole: _S = kA/4l_p^2_ (where S is entropy, k is Boltzmann’s constant, A is horizon area, l_p is Planck length).
