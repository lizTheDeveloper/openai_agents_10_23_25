# Black Hole Entropy and the Bekenstein Bound

## Black Hole Entropy
- According to the Bekenstein–Hawking formula, the entropy S of a black hole is proportional to the area (A) of its event horizon and not its volume.
- Formula:  
  S = (k * A) / (4 * l_p^2)
  
- Here, k is Boltzmann's constant, A is the event horizon area, and l_p is the Planck length.
- This result implies the fundamental degrees of freedom associated with a black hole live on its surface, not inside its volume.

## Bekenstein Bound
- The **Bekenstein bound** is a theoretical upper limit on the entropy (information) that can be contained within a given finite region of space with a finite amount of energy. 
- It suggests that maximum entropy is set by the area of the boundary, supporting the reasoning behind the holographic principle.

## Implications
- Provides the motivation for holography: information theory and black hole thermodynamics drive the idea that physical descriptions of regions are encoded on boundaries.
