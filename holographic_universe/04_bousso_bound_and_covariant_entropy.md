# Bousso Bound and Covariant Entropy Bound

## Bousso Bound (Covariant Entropy Bound)
- The **Bousso bound** is a generalization of the Bekenstein bound and the holographic principle to general spacetime settings, including dynamical and cosmological horizons.
- Proposed by Raphael Bousso, it refines earlier entropy bounds by specifying that the entropy passing through certain light-like (null) surfaces (called light-sheets) cannot exceed a quarter of the area of the surface from which the light-sheet is generated, measured in Planck units.
- This approach is "covariant" because it does not depend on a specific notion of time slice—unlike earlier, more naive bounds.

## Motivation and Importance
- Fixes the failures of the naive spatial entropy bound in cosmology and dynamical spacetime where the earlier form of the holographic bound doesn't hold.
- Applies generically, including for time-dependent or curved spacetimes (e.g., expanding universe).

## Formula (Informal)
- Entropy(S) ≤ (A/4 l_p^2) — the entropy flowing through the light-sheet S is no greater than one quarter its generating area in Planck units.
